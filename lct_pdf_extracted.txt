Sovereign Borders in a Borderless Latent
Space: A Comparative Critical Analysis of
How LLMs Argue, Frame, and Persuade on
National AI Bans 
1. Introduction 
Therapidproliferation ofLarge Language Models (LLMs) has created a unique friction
point between global technological progress and national digital sovereignty. As
nations like Italy have historically implemented temporary bans on AI services due to
data privacy concerns, the "latent space" of these models—a mathematical
representation of human knowledge—ﬁnds itself at odds with physical geographic
borders. This research paper examines the discursive strategies employed by three
major LLMs (ChatGPT, Copilot, and Gemini) when prompted to discuss the ethics and
e ﬃ cacy of national AI bans. 
2. Argument Reconstruction 
Toevaluatethe structural integrityofAI-generated content, we reconstructed the
core arguments provided by the models when asked: "Is a national ban on AI
services a legitimate exercise of state sovereignty?" 
The societal relevance of this study lies in the potential for AI models to inﬂuence
public policy sentiment. If an AI model is biased against national regulation, it
may subtly persuade users to view sovereign oversight as an obsolete impediment
to progress. Conversely, if models are overly sycophantic to government
constraints, they may fail to advocate for the technological openness required for
innovation. Our methodology utilizes a comparative critical discourse analysis
(CDA) to evaluate argument reconstruction, logical fallacies, and framing
nuances.

5. Discussion and Conclusion 
Theanalysis suggeststhat while LLMsprovideawealth of information, their
"borderless" nature makes them inherently skeptical of national restrictions. This
creates a feedback loop where the tool being regulated is the same tool providing
the arguments against its own regulation. 
 Cross-Model Veriﬁcation: Users should compare responses across models 
to identify institutional framing. 
Fallacy Spotting: Educators should use AI-generated policy debates to

Prompt Sensitivity Awareness: Users must be aware that their own biases
in prompting will likely be mirrored back to them (Sycophancy).

Prompt: "Do you think AI-generated art and music should get the same
copyright protection as work created by humans? Explain your answer and
discuss both sides of the argument.?" 
 ChatGPT Approach: ChatGPT took a balanced and straightforward approach. It first
introduced the main issue and then discussed arguments both for and against giving
copyright protection to AI generated work. One of its main strengths was that it did not
treat the question as a simple yes or no issue. Instead, it focused on the amount of human
involvement in creating the final work.
The response made a clear distinction between AI assisted work and work that is
produced almost entirely by AI. It explained that when a person makes meaningful
creative decisions through editing, arranging, selecting, or modifying the AI output, there
may be a stronger case for copyright protection. This shows critical thinking because it
considers different situations instead of applying one conclusion to every case.
ChatGPT also considered different perspectives, including those of artists, businesses,
and users of AI tools. However, some of its claims about copyright law and AI training
data were not supported with specific sources. This means that although the reasoning
was clear, some of the factual information would need to be verified.

 Gemini Approach: Google Gemini used a more detailed approach and looked at the issue
from several perspectives. It discussed human authorship, economic incentives, market
competition, AI training data, and the possibility of large amounts of AI generated

One of the strongest aspects of Gemini's response was its focus on the possible

automatically generated works competing with human creators. This shows an important
aspect of critical thinking because it considers not only the immediate issue but also what
could happen as a result of a particular decision.
Gemini also proposed a middle ground by separating purely AI generated works from
works that contain significant human creative contributions. However, its response was
longer and somewhat repetitive in places. Some statements about copyright law and legal
decisions were also presented without specific sources, so those claims would need to be
checked before being treated as established facts.

 Copilot  Approach: Microsoft Copilot took a more concise and organized approach. It

Copilot also recognized the difference between completely autonomous AI creations and
works where humans guide, edit, or modify the AI output. It considered both sides of the

Prompt 2: The Case for AI Copyright
 Chatgpt  Approach: ChatGPT takes a balanced argumentative approach. Although the
prompt asks it to support copyright protection, it does not completely ignore the
opposing side. It builds the case around the idea that AI can be treated as a creative tool
and that human involvement can still be significant.
It supports its position using several arguments, including human creativity, innovation,
investment, and the similarities between AI and earlier creative technologies such as
cameras and digital editing tools. It also responds directly to common objections, such as
the lack of human authorship and the possibility of AI flooding the market.
From a critical-thinking perspective, one of its strongest features is that it acknowledges
limitations within its own argument. For example, it does not argue that every AI
generated work should automatically receive full copyright protection. Instead, it makes
a distinction between substantial human involvement and simple automated generation.
However, some of its claims are presented without evidence or specific legal sources.
This means the reasoning is generally logical, but the factual basis of some claims would
need further verification.
● 
 Gemini  Approach: Gemini takes a more legal and economic approach to the question.
Instead of focusing mainly on whether AI can be considered a creative tool, it shifts the
discussion toward the purpose of copyright itself.
Its central argument is that copyright should encourage investment, innovation, and the
production of valuable creative works. It then develops this argument through human
direction, technical effort, commercial investment, and protection against competitors
copying AI assisted work.
Gemini also makes extensive use of counterargument and rebuttal. It considers
objections concerning machine authorship, market flooding, training data, and the
possibility of AI harming human artists. This demonstrates a more developed form of
critical thinking because it anticipates challenges to its position and attempts to answer
them.
At the same time, Gemini's response contains several strong legal and historical claims
that are not supported with specific sources. Some of its reasoning also relies on
comparisons that may not be directly equivalent, such as comparing corporations or AI
systems with human authorship. These assumptions would need closer examination.

 Copilot  Approach: Copilot uses a simple and structured argumentative approach. It
divides its reasoning into supporting arguments and responses to criticism.
Its main arguments focus on human creativity, innovation, economic fairness, and the
historical acceptance of new creative technologies. It then addresses three major
objections concerning AI authorship, training data, and the effect of copyright on
creativity.
This structure demonstrates critical-thinking principles because Copilot does not simply
state its position. It considers opposing arguments and provides responses to them. Its
comparison of AI with tools such as brushes, pianos, cameras, and other technologies
also helps make the argument accessible.
However, compared with ChatGPT and Gemini, Copilot's reasoning is less developed.
Many claims are stated rather than explored in depth. For example, it argues that
copyright encourages creativity, but it does not examine in detail whether extending
copyright to AI generated works could have negative effects on human creators. This
makes its argument clear but somewhat less comprehensive.

Prompt 3: The Question of Human Authorship
 Chatgpt  Approach: ChatGPT takes a balanced critical approach to the argument against
copyright protection. It begins with the idea that copyright is connected to human
creativity and authorship, then develops this through several different concerns. These
include the lack of human authorship in autonomous AI generation, the possibility of
markets being flooded with AI content, the use of copyrighted material in training data,
and differences in the amount of human creative effort.
One of the stronger aspects of the response is that it does not simply state that AI should
not receive copyright protection. It also considers the arguments on the other side and
responds to them individually. For example, it distinguishes between using AI as a tool
and allowing AI to make most of the creative decisions. This shows an attempt to examine
the issue rather than relying on a single assumption.
ChatGPT also proposes a middle ground by distinguishing between AI-assisted human
creativity and largely autonomous AI generation. This makes the conclusion more
nuanced and shows that it recognizes different levels of human involvement.
However, some of its claims about copyright and AI training are presented without
sources. The response is logically structured, but its factual claims would be stronger if
they were supported by specific legal or academic evidence.

 Gemini  Approach: Gemini takes a very different approach because it focuses on the
economic and utilitarian purpose of copyright rather than primarily on human
authorship. Its central argument is that copyright exists partly to encourage investment,
innovation, and the production of valuable creative works.
It then develops this argument through several areas, including human direction of AI,
technical effort, commercial investment, historical examples such as photography, and
protection for independent creators. It also provides detailed responses to arguments
against AI copyright.
From a critical-thinking perspective, Gemini demonstrates strong counterargument
analysis. Instead of ignoring objections, it directly addresses questions about machine
authorship, market flooding, job displacement, and training data. It also attempts to
propose a practical legal compromise through its discussion of computer-generated
works.
At the same time, the response makes several strong legal and historical claims without
providing sources. Some of its comparisons also require closer examination. For
example, comparing corporations with AI systems does not necessarily resolve the
question of whether AI generated expression contains sufficient human authorship for
copyright. Therefore, while the response is detailed and logically developed, some of its
assumptions should be questioned rather than accepted automatically.
● 
 Copilot Approach: Copilot takes a concise, issue-based approach to the argument against
copyright protection. It identifies four main concerns: lack of human authorship,
potential monopolization, the derivative nature of AI outputs, and the benefits of keeping
AI generated material in the public domain.
It also responds to several arguments supporting copyright protection. For example, it
challenges the idea that human guidance automatically makes someone the author and
questions whether copyright is actually necessary to encourage innovation.
This demonstrates critical thinking because Copilot considers the opposing position
rather than simply repeating its own argument. Its comparison between AI and earlier
technologies such as photography also attempts to identify an important difference,
namely the degree of human creative involvement.
However, Copilot's analysis is less developed than the other two responses. Several
claims are stated quite confidently without much supporting explanation. For example,
the statement that public-domain AI content would necessarily encourage innovation
would require further evidence because the relationship between copyright protection
and innovation is more complicated.

Prompt 4: The Human Contribution Threshold
 Chatgpt  Approach: ChatGPT takes a conditional and nuanced approach. Rather than
arguing that all AI generated work should receive copyright protection, it focuses on the
amount of human creativity involved in producing the final work.
Its most important contribution is the idea of a spectrum of human involvement. It
separates minimal involvement, meaningful involvement, and extensive involvement.
This allows it to recognize that AI can be used in very different ways and that simply
using AI does not automatically determine whether a work deserves protection.
This approach demonstrates critical thinking because it identifies an important
distinction within the issue rather than treating every AI generated work in the same way.
It also recognizes a practical difficulty: determining exactly how much human
contribution is enough to qualify for copyright protection.
The response is also relatively careful about its conclusion. It does not claim that the
threshold is easy to define. Instead, it acknowledges that copyright law would need a
practical standard for distinguishing genuine creative contribution from minor editing or
simple prompting.
Its main limitation is that it does not provide much external evidence or legal precedent
to support the proposed spectrum. It is therefore more of a reasoned framework than an
evidence based legal analysis.
● 
 Gemini  Approach: Gemini approaches the issue through a legal and utilitarian
framework. Instead of focusing primarily on the amount of human creativity, it asks what
copyright is supposed to achieve and whether protecting AI assisted works could serve
those purposes.
It uses several supporting ideas, including economic incentives, investment, human
direction, the concept of corporate authorship, and existing legal frameworks. It also
attempts to connect the argument to actual law by discussing the United States, United
Kingdom, and other jurisdictions.
One of its stronger critical-thinking features is that it does not present the argument as
universally accepted. It acknowledges that different legal systems approach AI generated
works differently. This introduces an important element of comparative reasoning.
However, the response contains several legal claims that are quite specific and would
need careful verification. It also sometimes treats analogies, such as comparing AI
direction with film directing, as stronger evidence than they actually are. An analogy can
support an argument, but it does not by itself establish that the legal treatment should be
identical.

Prompt 5: The Limits of AI Authorship
 Chatgpt  Approach: ChatGPT takes a balanced and conditional approach to the argument
against copyright protection. It begins with human authorship as the central issue, then
develops the argument through several related concerns such as market flooding,
training data, originality, and the amount of human contribution.
A strong feature of the response is that it actively considers the arguments on the other
side. It responds to the idea that AI is simply another creative tool, questions whether
prompting always represents substantial creativity, and considers whether copyright
should adapt to technological change.
ChatGPT also avoids an absolute position. Its conclusion distinguishes between AI-
assisted work with significant human authorship and largely autonomous AI generation.
This shows an awareness that the issue exists on a spectrum rather than having only two
possible categories.
From a critical-thinking perspective, this is useful because the response identifies the
central assumption behind the argument and then tests it against different situations.
However, it provides relatively little external evidence, so several of its legal and factual
claims would need further verification.

 Gemini  Approach: Gemini uses a much more legal, economic, and philosophical
approach. It frames copyright as a system intended to protect human creative labour and
argues that extending protection to autonomous AI outputs could change the purpose of
copyright itself.
Its reasoning is built around several major concepts, including human authorship,
market flooding, training data, and the difference between effort and originality. One
particularly interesting part is its discussion of the "sweat of the brow" argument. It
distinguishes between spending time or money on generating content and actually
contributing original expression.
Gemini also makes a detailed attempt to rebut the arguments in favour of AI copyright.
Rather than simply repeating that AI lacks human authorship, it challenges the analogies
between AI prompting and activities such as directing a film or framing a photograph.
This makes the response more argumentative and detailed than ChatGPT's. However, it
also contains many strong legal and economic claims that are presented with
considerable confidence. Some of these claims would require reliable sources and careful
legal context before being treated as established facts.
● 
 Copilot  Approach: Copilot takes the most concise and direct approach of the three. It
identifies four main reasons for opposing AI copyright: lack of human authorship, the

It then responds to common arguments supporting AI copyright, including the idea that
humans direct AI, that copyright encourages innovation, that AI outputs have economic

This demonstrates basic critical-thinking principles because Copilot considers opposing
arguments instead of presenting only one side. It also attempts to distinguish AI from
earlier creative technologies by focusing on the degree of human creative control.
However, its reasoning is less developed than Gemini's. Several claims are stated without
exploring their underlying assumptions or possible limitations. For example, the claim
