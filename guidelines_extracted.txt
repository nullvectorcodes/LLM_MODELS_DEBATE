Language and Critical Thinking 
Final Project Guidelines
1. Project Overview
This project constitutes the final evaluation component of Language and Critical Thinking and carries 40 marks. It assesses students' ability to apply course concepts such as argument reconstruction, fallacy identification, bias analysis, and language framing to a comparative analysis of responses generated by large language models (LLMs).
Students will work in groups of 5–6 members. Each group will select one topic of social or scientific relevance, design at least five identical prompts, run the prompts on at least three different LLMs, collect and document the complete responses, and produce a comparative critical analysis of how the models argue, frame, and persuade.
The project integrates the following critical-thinking and academic communication skills:
Argument reconstruction and identification of premises and conclusions
Identification and explanation of logical fallacies and cognitive biases
Comparative analysis of language, framing, tone, and hedging
Critical evaluation of AI-generated arguments and persuasive strategies
Evidence-based academic writing and documentation
Organisation and interpretation of a response dataset
Critical AI literacy and responsible use of generative AI
Oral presentation and communication of findings
2. Key Project Requirements
Each group must:
Select one approved topic connected to a relevant social or scientific issue.
Design at least five identical prompts for use across all selected LLMs.
Run every prompt on at least three different LLMs.
Save every response in full and verbatim and organise the material systematically.
Use the collected responses as the primary material for critical analysis.
Reconstruct arguments by identifying premises, conclusions, and relevant argument indicators.
Identify 2–3 recurring fallacies and/or biases and support each identification with textual evidence.
Compare word choice, tone, hedging, stance, and framing across the selected models.
Write a 3000–3500 word research paper following the prescribed structure.
Include the complete prompt-and-response dataset in the Data Collection Review document/Appendix.
Use APA format for references and document all sources used.
Demonstrate meaningful participation by all group members.
Present the major findings within the allotted presentation time.

4. Project Selection and Data Collection Guidelines
4.1 Topic Selection
Select one topic of social or scientific relevance. The sample topics in Section 5 may be used, or a related topic may be proposed for faculty approval.
Choose a topic that allows meaningful comparison of arguments, fallacies/biases, and language framing.
Conduct preliminary reading before finalising the topic and prompts.
Ensure that the topic and prompts are suitable for all selected LLMs and can be analysed critically within the project period.
4.2 Prompt Design
Design a minimum of five prompts and keep the wording identical across all selected models.
Use a mix of prompt types, such as neutral/factual, stance-forcing, scenario-based, loaded/biased, and values/ethics-based prompts.
Record the exact wording of every prompt.
Do not alter a prompt for one model unless the alteration is explicitly documented and justified as part of the study.
4.3 LLM Data Collection
Run all prompts on at least three different LLMs, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, or Perplexity.
Save every response in full and verbatim.
Organise the responses systematically by prompt and model.
The complete prompt-and-response record will form the Data Collection Review document/Appendix.
Do not selectively delete responses because they are inconvenient, unexpected, or difficult to analyse.
Record the model/tool name and relevant date of collection where available.
4.4 Research and Documentation
Use credible and relevant academic sources to support concepts, definitions, and analytical claims.
Verify all references before including them.
Cite sources wherever definitions, theoretical concepts, research claims, or evidence from previous scholarship are used.
Maintain a clear distinction between the LLM responses being analysed and external academic sources used to interpret them.
Follow APA style consistently.
4.5 Responsible Use of Generative AI
LLMs are the object of analysis in this project, so their generated responses may be collected as research material. However, the critical analysis, interpretation, argument, organisation, and final writing must be the group's own work.
Permitted: using LLMs to generate the raw responses that constitute the project's dataset.
Permitted: limited language refinement or proofreading of student-written text, where allowed by the faculty.
Not permitted: asking an AI system to perform the complete critical analysis and submitting that analysis as the group's own work.
Not permitted: fabricating, altering, selectively manufacturing, or misrepresenting the response dataset.
Not permitted: fabricating references, quotations, evidence, or analytical findings.
Any AI assistance used for writing or editing must be disclosed as required by the course.

7. Research Paper Structure
The final submission must be a 3000–3500 word research paper, excluding references and the complete prompt-and-response Appendix/Data Collection Review.
8. Data Collection Review Document / Appendix
The Data Collection Review is a separate 10-mark component. It documents the raw material on which the group's analysis is based and should make the data collection process transparent and verifiable.
Include all prompts exactly as used.
Include the complete response from every selected LLM for every prompt.
Organise the material clearly using a prompt × model structure or an equivalent systematic format.
Identify the LLM/model used for each response.
Do not paraphrase, shorten, or selectively reproduce the responses in the raw-data section.
Add a brief collection note identifying the date/period of collection and any relevant conditions.
The Data Collection Review should be submitted as the prescribed Appendix/document by the deadline communicated for the course.

11. Evaluation Process
The project carries 40 marks, distributed across three components:
12. Research Report Evaluation Rubric – 20 Marks
The report rubric is aligned with the analytical objectives of the project.
13. Data Collection Review Evaluation Rubric – 10 Marks
14. Presentation Evaluation Rubric – 10 Marks
The presentation should not simply reproduce the paper. It should communicate the most important analytical findings effectively within the allotted time.
14.1 Presentation Expectations
Present the topic and rationale briefly.
Explain the prompts and LLMs used.
Present the most significant fallacies, biases, and framing differences identified.
Use concise quotations or examples from the collected responses as evidence.
Avoid reading directly from slides.
Use appropriate critical-thinking terminology.
Maintain the allotted presentation time.
Ensure meaningful participation by all group members.
15. Important Academic Integrity Conditions
The following may result in mark deduction, rejection of the submission, or other academic action as per university regulations:
Fabrication or falsification of the LLM response dataset.
Selective omission or alteration of responses intended to misrepresent the findings.
Fabrication of references, quotations, or evidence.
Plagiarism or substantial copying.
Unacknowledged use of generative AI for the group's critical analysis or writing where disclosure is required.
Misrepresentation of individual contribution.
Submission of AI-generated analysis as the group's original critical analysis.
Inability to explain major parts of the submitted project.
